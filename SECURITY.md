# Security Policy

## Reporting a vulnerability

**Please do not open a public issue for a security vulnerability.** A public
report is visible to everyone, including people running this software, before
anyone has had a chance to ship a fix.

Report privately through either channel:

- **GitHub private vulnerability reporting** — open the repository's **Security**
  tab and choose **Report a vulnerability**. This is preferred: it keeps the
  report, the discussion, and the eventual advisory in one place.
- **Email** — <security@outerlayer.ai>. If you want to encrypt, say so in a
  first message and we will send a key.

Please include, as far as you are able:

- What an attacker can do, and what they need in order to do it (an
  unauthenticated request? an account in another tenant? an existing member with
  a low-privilege role?).
- The affected component — dashboard, gateway, CLI, self-host, a published
  package — and a commit or version if you know it.
- Steps to reproduce, ideally the smallest ones that work. A `curl` command or a
  failing test is worth more than a description.
- Whether you are reporting against your own deployment, our hosted service, or
  a source read.

## What to expect

| | Target |
| --- | --- |
| We acknowledge your report | within 3 business days |
| We give you an initial assessment (severity, whether we can reproduce it) | within 10 business days |
| We ship a fix, or explain why we are not going to | tracked with you until closed |

If a report is critical and we can reproduce it, we will prioritise it over
feature work. We will tell you when a fix ships and, unless you would rather
stay anonymous, credit you in the advisory and the release notes.

## Scope

**In scope** — anything in this repository, and our hosted service:

- Cross-tenant data access, in either direction, by any route.
- Privilege escalation within a tenant, including a member gaining permissions
  their role does not carry.
- Authentication or session flaws: token handling, API-key verification, SSO.
- Anything that lets a tenant's own agent code reach another tenant, or reach
  the control plane.
- Supply-chain issues in the published `outerlayer` CLI or `@outerlayer/*`
  packages.

**Out of scope** — we will close these as informational:

- Findings that require an already-compromised `service_role` key, a stolen
  session, or physical access to a developer's machine.
- Missing hardening headers or a TLS-configuration grade with no demonstrated
  impact.
- Denial of service by brute volume against our hosted endpoints. Resource
  exhaustion achievable by **one tenant against another** is in scope — please
  report that.
- Bypassing the Enterprise license check. `ee/` is source-available; the licence
  gate is a commercial control, not a security boundary. If bypassing it also
  disables a security control, that part **is** in scope.
- Reports generated by a scanner with no verification of exploitability.

## Testing rules

Please test against **your own** local or self-hosted deployment. Two routes get
you one:

- **A development checkout** — [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md) has the
  sequence. `npx supabase start` in `apps/tenant-dashboard` brings up the
  database and auth; ClickHouse comes from the compose file in
  `apps/tenant-dashboard/clickhouse/`.
- **A self-hosted instance** — the self-hosting documentation covers standing
  one up, and `apps/gateway-node/README.md` covers running the gateway outside
  Cloudflare.

Note that a self-hosted instance is not configured identically to our hosted
service, so tell us which one you tested against — it changes whether a finding
reproduces for us.

If you need to test against our hosted service, use your own account and your
own data, and email us first. Do not access another customer's data, run
volumetric tests, or attempt social engineering against our staff or vendors. If
you access another tenant's data by accident while validating a finding, stop,
tell us what you saw, and do not retain it — we will treat that as good-faith
research.

## Safe harbour

We will not pursue or support legal action against anyone who makes a good-faith
effort to follow this policy. If a third party brings action against you for
research conducted in line with it, we will make it known that your work was
authorised.

There is no paid bounty programme today. We are a small team and we would rather
be honest about that than imply otherwise.

## Fixed issues

Published advisories, once we have any, appear under the repository's **Security
Advisories**. Fixes ship in normal releases, called out in the changelog.
